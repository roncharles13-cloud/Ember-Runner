WiFi-Sense · how it perceives

A one-pixel camera
that samples very fast

People picture WiFi sensing as an X-ray that draws a body on a screen. On a plain laptop it is almost the opposite. A camera records millions of points arranged in space. WiFi RSSI records one number arranged in time. Here is what that actually looks like — built from the real 46-second capture you just ran.

The core difference

Space versus time

A webcam spends its resolution on where — a grid of pixels that together form a shape. RSSI has no grid at all. It spends everything on when — one value, over and over, very fast.

A camera sees
~921,600pixels, arranged in 2D space
vs
RSSI sees
1value, no position at all

That single pixel has no up, no down, no left, no right. It cannot form a shape, because a shape needs at least two pixels to compare. All it can do is get brighter or dimmer as the room's radio conditions change.

The whole recording

Everything it captured in 46 seconds

So here is the sensor's entire "image" of the room — the one pixel's brightness written left-to-right as time. It is exactly one pixel tall. There is no second row to find a body in.

Why only one pixel

The room collapses onto a single link

The laptop hears the router along essentially one path. A body moving in that path nudges the received strength — but the measurement can't tell where on the path, or what shape caused it. Everything in the room folds down to that one number.

Where the "detail" comes from

It reads the flicker, not a picture

Because space is gone, time is everything. The software watches how fast and how much that one pixel flickers. Three patterns fall out of the same stripe above — and that's the whole trick.

The takeaway: WiFi RSSI sensing isn't a blurry camera — it's a one-pixel light meter read hundreds of times a second. It perceives the room the way you'd perceive a party through a single dimmer switch in another room: you can tell someone's moving around, maybe hear a rhythm — but you'll never see the room. Adding CSI hardware is what turns that one pixel into dozens (per-subcarrier), which is the first real step toward anything picture-like. On the laptop, python -m wifi_sense is that one pixel, live.

WiFi-Sense · latent axes

Two new dimensions,
from the same one pixel

The sensor still measures one number. But math can unfold that 1D stream into extra axes it never physically recorded. Here are the two we added — a spectrogram (time × frequency) and a delay-embedding phase portrait — computed live from the very same 46-second capture. No new hardware. Just a short-time Fourier transform and Takens' theorem.

Latent axis #1 · frequency

The spectrogram: what kind of motion, and when

Slide a little FFT along the signal and stack the slices. Now movement sorts itself by speed: breathing sits in a low band, limbs higher, walking sprays broadband. A flat line becomes a picture of behaviour over time.

Latent axis #2 · reconstructed state

The phase portrait: the shape of what's happening

Takens' trick: plot the signal against a delayed copy of itself — s(t) vs s(t−τ). A 1D wiggle unfolds into a 2D trajectory whose shape is the state. Same three phases, same delay (τ = 18 samples), three unmistakably different shapes:

EMPTY ROOM
Collapses to a dot — no dynamics, nothing moving.
BREATHING
Traces a clean loop — a periodic limit cycle. That ring is respiration.
WALKING
Smears into a cloud — high-energy, aperiodic, filling the plane.
The compact readout

Three bands, one glance

Collapse the spectrogram into three numbers — breathing / limb / fast energy — and you can watch the room's mode shift in real time.

The point: none of this is new signal — it's the same one pixel, reshaped. Frequency and reconstructed-state are axes hiding inside the timing of a single number, and pulling them out is pure software. This is exactly what runs live now: python -m wifi_sense shows the scrolling spectrogram and phase portrait in the dashboard. It's the ceiling of what one RSSI stream can reveal — richer views, not a richer sensor. A picture of the room still needs CSI hardware feeding the very same pipeline.
